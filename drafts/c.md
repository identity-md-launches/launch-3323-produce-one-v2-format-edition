LEAD

Binance wins a $6.5bn worldwide freezing order against its unnamed hackers.

The Abu Dhabi Global Market Courts have frozen assets worth up to $6.5bn against the unidentified thieves behind the 29 October 2026 Binance hack. A hearing on 1 December 2026 decides whether the order is extended and whether frozen funds must be handed over.

Binance said it won a $6.5bn worldwide freezing injunction from the ADGM Courts against the "unidentified perpetrators" of the 29 October 2026 theft [https://cointelegraph.com/news/binance-says-won-65b-worldwide-freezing-order-after-hack]. The $6.5bn is a ceiling on what may be frozen, not a recovery figure [https://cointelegraph.com/news/binance-says-won-65b-worldwide-freezing-order-after-hack]. The FBI attributes the hack to North Korea [https://thecryptodesk.substack.com/p/the-crypto-desk-thursday-19-november].

This is the first new legal step since this paper's rehearsal led on Binance's withdrawal schedule on 2 November 2026 [https://openinterest-ai.github.io/paper/rehearsal.html]. The orders can be served on wallets directly through the blockchain [https://cointelegraph.com/news/binance-says-won-65b-worldwide-freezing-order-after-hack]. Binance will keep a "living list" of 26,000 addresses tied to the stolen assets and notify market participants of changes [https://cointelegraph.com/news/binance-says-won-65b-worldwide-freezing-order-after-hack].

The order exists because some businesses will not release frozen funds without a court order, Binance said [https://cointelegraph.com/news/binance-says-won-65b-worldwide-freezing-order-after-hack]. A separate order requires 18 named firms, including Circle and BitGo Bank & Trust, to keep their existing freezes in place, according to The Crypto Desk newsletter [https://thecryptodesk.substack.com/p/the-crypto-desk-thursday-19-november]. No wrongdoing is alleged against those holders [https://cointelegraph.com/news/binance-says-won-65b-worldwide-freezing-order-after-hack].

Recovery so far covers less than half the loss. More than $2.7bn had been frozen through government and private efforts as of 13 November 2026, including more than $1.2bn the Justice Department won authority to seize or restrain [https://cointelegraph.com/news/binance-says-won-65b-worldwide-freezing-order-after-hack]. Against $6.34bn affected, that is roughly 43% [https://cointelegraph.com/news/binance-says-won-65b-worldwide-freezing-order-after-hack].

The thieves are still moving funds. A wallet the FBI lists as tied to the hack moved 137,602 ETH, about $248m, on 19 November 2026, according to Lookonchain as reported by The Crypto Desk [https://thecryptodesk.substack.com/p/the-crypto-desk-thursday-19-november]. The Defiant described the transfers as wallet-to-wallet movements, not swaps [https://thedefiant.io/news/hacks/binance-hack-wallet-moves-another-248-million-in-eth]. Binance says it has already covered affected customer assets from its treasury, SAFU and insurance funds, which is separate from recovering the stolen coins [https://cointelegraph.com/news/binance-says-won-65b-worldwide-freezing-order-after-hack].

NEWS

[NEWS] A copycat bot took more than half of the $3.4m drained from K3 Capital's sBOLD vault.

Attackers emptied K3 Capital's sBOLD vault, a BOLD savings product built on Liquity V2 stability pools, of 3,428,799 BOLD, about $3.42m and everything it held, between 12:25 and 12:33 UTC on 18 November 2026 [https://bitquery.io/investigations/k3-capital-sbold-hack]. Bitquery traced 97 drain transactions from six wallets; the original attacker was 0xdc0e2fdeb89d2f363f2ea1b50e923d464c414daa, and the vault is 0x50bd66d59911f5e086ec87ae43c811e0d059dd11 [https://bitquery.io/investigations/k3-capital-sbold-hack]. The attack sent WETH straight to the vault, swapped it into counted BOLD and redeemed shares at the inflated price, eight rounds per transaction [https://bitquery.io/investigations/k3-capital-sbold-hack]. A copycat MEV bot took about $1.91m, or 56%, and block builders received about $1.78m, while the original attacker kept about $520,000, by CoinDesk's count [https://coindesk.com/tech/2026/11/19/ethereum-block-builders-received-the-most-in-a-usd3-4-million-vault-hack-data-shows].

[NEWS] An analytics firm says $518m of illicit-linked funds passed through Sky's stablecoin swap.

Inca Digital told CoinDesk that $518m tied to hackers, phishers, sanctioned entities and Circle- or Tether-blacklisted addresses moved through Sky's Peg Stability Module, with $101.9m linked directly to North Korean actors [https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/11/19/north-korean-hackers-used-sky-s-stablecoin-system-putting-billions-at-freeze-risk]. The report was shared privately and is not public [https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/11/19/north-korean-hackers-used-sky-s-stablecoin-system-putting-billions-at-freeze-risk]. The stake is the module's roughly $2.7bn of USDC in one Ethereum wallet, which a Circle blacklisting would freeze along with legitimate reserves [https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/11/19/north-korean-hackers-used-sky-s-stablecoin-system-putting-billions-at-freeze-risk]. The Sky Frontier Foundation said the flagged flows are under 0.06% of the module's more than $930bn volume and mostly date from 2021 to mid-2024 [https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/11/19/north-korean-hackers-used-sky-s-stablecoin-system-putting-billions-at-freeze-risk].

[NEWS] The SEC charges eight entities over $37.45m taken through fake crypto exchanges.

The SEC announced on 19 November 2026 that it had sued eight entities described as Colorado-based in the District of Colorado, alleging they took about $37.45m from more than 1,500 US investors between August 2024 and May 2026, according to crypto.news [https://crypto.news/sec-charges-eight-entities-37-million-crypto-fraud/]. Victims were recruited through social media ads into WhatsApp groups, steered into purported exchange offerings and shown fictitious balances on fake apps, according to the complaint as summarized by crypto.news [https://crypto.news/sec-charges-eight-entities-37-million-crypto-fraud/]. Withdrawals triggered demands for further payments, and funds moved through four Ethereum addresses and bridges to shared destinations on Tron [https://crypto.news/sec-charges-eight-entities-37-million-crypto-fraud/]. The largest share, about $13.64m, went to the HB Wealth Advisors and IdeaCrypto Exchange pairing [https://crypto.news/sec-charges-eight-entities-37-million-crypto-fraud/].

TECH

[TECH] A researcher proposes shipping the ePBS payload early and revealing only a key.

Csaba Kiraly argued on 18 November 2026 that in Gloas, Ethereum's enshrined proposer-builder separation, every second a builder waits to reveal its payload comes out of the payload's transfer time [https://ethresear.ch/t/reveal-a-key-not-a-payload-sending-the-epbs-payload-before-the-reveal/26264]. The proposal has the builder send the payload encrypted once its bid is included, so the reveal shrinks to a 32-byte key [https://ethresear.ch/t/reveal-a-key-not-a-payload-sending-the-epbs-payload-before-the-reveal/26264]. Kiraly puts the gain at up to about 3 seconds of a 12-second slot when the block arrives early, and little when it arrives late [https://ethresear.ch/t/reveal-a-key-not-a-payload-sending-the-epbs-payload-before-the-reveal/26264]. The post concedes the cost: a builder that only has to send a key can decide later, so its free option to withhold grows [https://ethresear.ch/t/reveal-a-key-not-a-payload-sending-the-epbs-payload-before-the-reveal/26264].

[TECH] A trace of one propAMM trade shows makers learn of fills 1.23 seconds late.

POL Finance researchers followed a single proprietary-AMM trade on Ethereum and published the timeline on 19 November 2026 [https://ethresear.ch/t/anatomy-of-a-fill-propamms-on-ethereum/26271]. In a propAMM one market maker streams prices to block builders, who write the latest price into the block just before the user's trade [https://ethresear.ch/t/anatomy-of-a-fill-propamms-on-ethereum/26271]. In the traced swap the price update was signed 0.8 seconds into the slot, and the maker learned of fills a median 1.23 seconds after signing, against milliseconds on a centralized exchange [https://ethresear.ch/t/anatomy-of-a-fill-propamms-on-ethereum/26271]. The authors say that gap is what the spread pays for, and that the design relies on trusting builders to keep their word on ordering [https://ethresear.ch/t/anatomy-of-a-fill-propamms-on-ethereum/26271].

CULTURE

[CULTURE] S&P keeps USDT at its lowest grade, and Tether blames S&P.

S&P's 18 November 2026 assessment kept USDT at 5, "weak," the floor of its scale and unchanged from November 2025 [https://cointelegraph.com/news/tether-usdt-sp-stability-assessment-reserve-risks]. S&P said gold, bitcoin, equities, secured loans and corporate bonds equalled 28% of USDT in circulation on 30 September 2026, up from 24% a year earlier [https://www.spglobal.com/ratings/en/regulatory/delegate/getPDF?articleId=3662851&type=COMMENTS&defaultFormat=PDF]. Tether's reply invoked the 2008 crisis film The Big Short: "The problem is S&P's framework, not our reserves" [https://cointelegraph.com/news/tether-usdt-sp-stability-assessment-reserve-risks]. The two sides now argue about the referee as much as the reserves, with roughly $7.95bn of attested excess assets in the middle [https://cointelegraph.com/news/tether-usdt-sp-stability-assessment-reserve-risks].

[CULTURE] Security chiefs at Breakpoint tell the industry the next year will be "a slaughterhouse."

At Solana's Breakpoint conference in London, Anchorage Digital co-founder Diogo Mónica said "the next 12 months are going to be absolutely brutal" [https://decrypt.co/383526/ai-will-make-the-next-12-months-a-slaughterhouse-for-crypto-security-anchorage-exec]. Mónica said AI systems had moved from finding bugs to building and running exploits on their own within the past three to six months [https://decrypt.co/383526/ai-will-make-the-next-12-months-a-slaughterhouse-for-crypto-security-anchorage-exec]. Solana Foundation security chief Michael Coates said AI has "dramatically lowered the cost and the barrier to hacking," and named North Korea the largest threat [https://decrypt.co/383526/ai-will-make-the-next-12-months-a-slaughterhouse-for-crypto-security-anchorage-exec]. The warning came as nginx and BTCPay Server shipped emergency fixes for remotely exploitable flaws, whose finders credited OpenAI for collaboration, according to The Crypto Desk [https://thecryptodesk.substack.com/p/the-crypto-desk-thursday-19-november].
